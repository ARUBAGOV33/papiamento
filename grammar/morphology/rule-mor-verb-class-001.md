## Rule

Morfologia divides verbs into independent verbs, which work on their own, and dependent verbs, which need an independent verb or another word class to work in a sentence. Independent verbs are transitive or intransitive, and also personal or impersonal; some are reflexive. Dependent verbs are auxiliaries (modal or passive) or copulative verbs; irregular verbs, a limited group that does not take the particle ta or uses ta or a irregularly, are treated separately.

## Source

Manual di Gramatica di Papiamento: Morfologia (2010), §6.2 "Clasificacion" and §6.2.1 "Verbo independiente", p. 35; §6.2.1.5 "Verbo reflexivo" and §6.2.2 "Verbo dependiente", p. 37; §6.2.2.1 "Verbo auxiliar" and §6.2.2.2 "Verbo copulativo", pp. 37-38; §6.2.3 "Verbo iregular", p. 39. "Ta distingui dos subcategoria di verbo: verbo independiente y verbo dependiente."

## Correct

- Modal verbs (p. 37): gusta, kier, mag (di), mester, por, sa, tin cu/tin di
- Passive auxiliaries (p. 38): wordo y ser
- Copulative verbs (p. 38): bira, keda, mustra, parce, sinti, ta y yama
- Verbs that do not take ta (p. 39): conoce, ke/kier, mag (di), mester (di), por, sa(bi), ta y tin(i)

## Notes

- p. 37 prints the last modal as "tin cu/" at a line end followed by "tin di". p. 38 adds that keda is also used as a passive auxiliary ("Tambe sa uza keda como verbo auxiliar pasivo").
- One verb can belong to several classes depending on the sentence; the book's example is keda, which can be independent, modal, passive auxiliary or copulative (§6.2.2.2 Nota 1, p. 38).
- Ruling conflict: the invariant says modals (kier, mester, por, sa, gusta, mag) never take ta (_meta/invariants.md; RULE_GRA_MODAL_TA_001). §6.2.3.2 (p. 39) instead lists gusta among verbs used with or without ta ("Mucha (ta) gusta core den un espacio grandi."). The ruling wins for the checker. There is no errata row for this yet; a reviewer may want to add one.
