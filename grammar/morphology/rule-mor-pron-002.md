## Rule

ele is the emphatic form of the personal pronoun e, and can also be a variant of e. After a verb that can take both an indirect and a direct object, ele can also stand for the two objects together. For third-person possession, the possessive su or nan with its noun can be placed directly after the possessor instead of using the prepositional form with di; this combination is also used to show respect and courtesy to the person addressed.

## Source

Morfologia, §7.2.1.1 E pronomber personal ele (pp. 54–55): "E forma ele tin dos funcion." §7.2.2.1 E pronombernan posesivo su y nan (p. 56), with its Nota.

## Correct

- Ta ele a yama mi, no ta ami a yam’e.
- Maria a dun’ele cinco carchi pa cuminda.
- Alicia a dun’e¹ e² → Alicia a dun’ele
- E barba di papa. / Papa su barba.
- E buki di e muchanan. / E muchanan nan buki.
- “Señora, señora su cedula a cay”, e mucha homber a bisa (forma di respet/cortesia).

## Notes

- In the dun’e¹ e² example, e¹ is the indirect object (Gregorio) and e² the direct object (e buki), as the page explains.
- Other special forms of e on p. 54: e becomes el when the particle a follows (Nota 1), and e changes form after some prepositions, e.g. cu ne, di dje, na ne or na dje (Nota 2). Those are covered by the orthography and preposition rules (e.g. RULE_ORT_039, RULE_PRE_CU_NE_001).
- Nota 3 on p. 54 marks as incorrect the use of e instead of a demonstrative to refer back to a previous sentence ("*E ta un asunto di interes nacional" → "Esaki ta un asunto di interes nacional"). It has no rule file in this batch.
- _meta/invariants.md: collective nouns are singular and take su.
