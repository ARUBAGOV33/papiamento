## Rule

Conjunctions fall into two subcategories: coordinating and subordinating. Coordinating conjunctions are copulative, disjunctive or adversative; subordinating conjunctions are conditional, final (purpose), causal, consecutive, temporal, concessive or completive.

## Source

Morfologia, §8.3 Clasificacion y uzo, p. 61: "Ta parti conhuncion den dos subcategoria: conhuncion di coordinacion y conhuncion di subordinacion." §8.3.1 (p. 61) and §8.3.2 (p. 62) give the subtypes.

## Correct

- Copulative: y, ni, ni … ni, tambe, cu (p. 61)
- Disjunctive: o/of, sea ... sea (p. 62)
- Adversative: ma, pero, sino, toch, sinembargo (p. 62)
- Mi tanta cu mi mama a bay les di cushinamento na YMCA.

## Notes

- §8.2 Forma (p. 61): conjunctions are simple (toch, ma) or compound (prome cu, di manera cu).
- The Nota on p. 62 says pero can always replace sino, but sino can be used only when the main clause is negative. It has no rule file in this batch.
- The subordinating subtypes are described one by one from §8.3.2.1 (p. 63).
- The old rule pack used this as a lexicon convention (tag conjunction subtypes); nothing is flagged.
