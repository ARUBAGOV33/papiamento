## Rule
The predicate is the sentence part that expresses the eventuality attributed to the subject: an action the subject performs, a process it goes through, or a state it is in. The predicate is the nucleus of the sentence, its central part; all other sentence parts relate to it and sometimes depend on it. The predicate is formed by one or more verbs, generally with a particle in front of the (first) verb; the verb is the nucleus of the predicate.

## Source
Manual di Gramatica di Papiamento: Sintaxis (2015), §2.2.1 "Definicion", p. 24, and §2.2.2 "Structura", p. 25.
"E predicado ta e nucleo di e frase, e parti di frase central."
"E verbo ta e nucleo di e predicado y e particula ta sirbi pa expresa tempo, modo y aspecto di e verbo."

## Correct
- E mucha muhe tabata bula di un banda pa otro. (accion)
- E matanan a crece masha lihe. (proceso)
- E dos muchanan ta parce nan mama. (estado)

## Notes
- Severity is info: this is a definition for analysis, nothing to flag.
- In the examples the book marks tabata bula, a crece and ta parce nan mama as the predicate; tabata, a and ta are the particles.
- The old pack added that "serial or coordinated clauses may have more than one predicate". The book does show coordinated predicates, e.g. E ta yora of (ta) rabia cada rato. (§3.1.2.1, p. 68; see RULE_SYN_COORD_001), and treats serial verbs in §2.2.4.1 (II.3).
- §1.3 (p. 6) states the same point briefly; see RULE_SYN_SENT_002.
- The Nota on p. 25 links actions, processes and states to transitive, intransitive and copular verbs (Morfologia §6.2.1.1–6.2.1.2).
