## Rule
A sentence part (parti di frase) is a word or a combination of words that functions independently in the sentence; its function determines the meaning of the sentence. That sentence parts can be moved around the sentence shows that they are independent of each other. Besides subject and predicate, the sentence parts are objects (direct, indirect, prepositional) and complements (adverbial, subject complement, object complement); complements can be left out without harming the grammaticality of the sentence.

## Source
Manual di Gramatica di Papiamento: Sintaxis (2015), §2.1 "Introduccion", pp. 23–24.
"Un parti di frase ta un palabra of un combinacion di palabra cu ta funciona independientemente den frase; e funcion cu e parti di frase tin ta determinante pa nificacion di e frase"

## Correct
- E cacho ta drumi.
- E cacho di bisiña ta drumi.
- Indira ta drumi.
- Tur diasabra Indira ta drumi te diesdos or.
- Tur diasabra te diesdos or Indira ta drumi.
- Te diesdos or tur diasabra Indira ta drumi.
- Te diesdos or Indira ta drumi tur diasabra.

## Notes
- Severity is info: this defines the units the other syntax rules talk about. Nothing to flag.
- The last four examples show the complements Tur diasabra and te diesdos or moved to different places around Indira ta drumi.
- Objects are more closely tied to the predicate; complements add detail and can be dropped (p. 24).
- p. 24 also says the adjectival complement (complemento adhetival) is not a sentence part, because it cannot function independently; the book treats it under relative clauses (§4.2.3).
