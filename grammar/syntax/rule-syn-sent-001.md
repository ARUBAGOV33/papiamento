## Rule
Sintaxis calls an utterance a sentence (frase) when, in its basic form, it consists of a subject and a predicate, or of a predicate alone. An utterance with no predicate is not a sentence but a clause (clausula); the book names interjections, answers, greetings, farewells and certain sayings and proverbs as examples.

## Source
Manual di Gramatica di Papiamento: Sintaxis (2015), §1.2.1 "Frase", p. 5, and §1.2.2 "Clausula", p. 6.
"Nos ta denomina frase e enunciado cu den su forma basico ta consisti di suheto y predicado of solamente di predicado."
"Nos ta denomina clausula e enunciado cu no tin predicado."

## Correct
- Subject and predicate: E mucha a grita yora.
- Subject and predicate: E cas ta grandi.
- Subject and predicate: Tur hende ta soda.
- Predicate only: Bay!
- Predicate only: Ta claro!
- Clause, no predicate: Mi mama dushi!
- Clause, no predicate: Anos si.
- Clause, no predicate: Bon dia.
- Clause, no predicate: Te otro siman.
- Clause, no predicate: Barica yen, curason contento.

## Notes
- Severity is info. The old rule pack made this an error and told the checker to reject fragments without a predicate. The book does not support that: §1.2.2 treats utterances without a predicate (clausula) as a normal kind of utterance, so a missing predicate is not a mistake in itself.
- The old pack said imperatives may be predicate-only. The page's predicate-only examples are Bay! and Ta claro!; it does not limit the predicate-only case to imperatives.
