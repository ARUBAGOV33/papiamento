## Rule
The basic structure of a sentence is subject – predicate, and the subject generally precedes the predicate. The usual order of sentence parts is (complement) – subject – predicate – object – (complement), which is why linguistics classifies Papiamento as an SVO language.

## Source
Manual di Gramatica di Papiamento: Sintaxis (2015), §1.3 "Structura", p. 6.
"E structura basico di frase ta: suheto - predicado. E caracteristica principal di e structura aki ta cu e suheto generalmente ta precede e predicado."
"Generalmente e secuencia di e partinan di frase na Papiamento ta: (complemento) - suheto - predicado - obheto - (complemento)."

## Correct
- E mucha a grita yora.
- E cas ta grandi.
- Tur hende ta soda.

## Notes
- Severity is info. The book says "generalmente" and prints no wrong order, so there is nothing for the checker to flag. The old rule pack's warning ("flag unusual inversion for review") is not on the page.
- The examples are from §1.2.1 on the same page (p. 6), where the book marks the subject and the predicate.
- Constructions where a part other than the subject comes first are described elsewhere in the book, e.g. ta enfatico (§1.5, from p. 15) and fronted negated parts (RULE_SYN_NEG_003). Complements can move around the sentence (RULE_SYN_PART_001).
