## Rule
A subject complement specifies or qualifies the subject in a sentence whose predicate has an independent verb as its nucleus, never a copular verb. When a copular verb is the nucleus of the predicate and specifies or qualifies the subject, the sentence has a nominal predicate, and the qualifying word is the nominal part of the predicate (parti nominal di e predicado), not a subject complement.

## Source
Manual di Gramatica di Papiamento: Sintaxis (2015), §2.8.1 "Definicion", Nota, p. 62; §2.8.3 "Complemento di suheto", p. 63; §2.8.4 "Diferencia entre predicado nominal y predicado verbal cu complemento di suheto", p. 64.
"e verbo cu ta uza den e predicado como nucleo semper ta un verbo independiente y no por ta nunca un verbo copulativo"

## Correct
- Subject complement: Semper Melisa tabata cana bay scol masha contento.
- Subject complement: Cu su pia den gips e obrero a drenta e fabrica.
- Subject complement: Masha preocupa Chepi a skirbi e carta pa Hues.
- Subject complement: Yen di admiracion Stan a para contempla e cuadranan.
- Nominal predicate (copular verb bira): Su ruman a bira dokter.

## Notes
- Severity is info: this is a distinction for analysis. The book prints no wrong sentence, and the old pack's warning has nothing to flag in a text.
- In Su ruman a bira dokter., a is the particle, bira the copular verb and dokter the noun; a bira is the verbal part and dokter the nominal part of the predicate (p. 64). In Masha preocupa Chepi a skirbi e carta pa Hues., skirbi is an independent verb, so masha preocupa is a subject complement that relates directly to Chepi (p. 64).
- The copular verbs listed in Morfologia §6.2.2.2 (MOR2010 p. 38) are bira, keda, mustra, parce, sinti, ta and yama. Nominal predicate: Sintaxis §2.2.4.2, p. 34.
- Subject and object complements together are called complemento predicativo (p. 62). Object complement: RULE_SYN_COMP_OBJ_001.
